TBT: Early days of Spanish nuclear

The first major analysis of Spain’s nuclear sector in the archives of Nuclear News came in the September 1968 issue, only a few months before the February 1969 commercial startup of the country’s first nuclear power plant: José Cabrera, a 142-MWe PWR. That analysis opens with a pithy subheading summing up the state of that burgeoning industry: “Big uranium deposits spur country to full program.” Indeed, while its first nuclear power plant had yet to enter commercial operation, by 1968, the Spanish nuclear power program was already a multifaceted and complex one. NN details that the Junta de Energía Nuclear (JEN, the singular national nuclear agency in Spain) was embarking on “a constant search” for new technologies in three fields: The fuel cycle, from the prospecting for and mining of uranium to the final treatment of irradiated fuels. Electronics, especially in the domain of the development of instruments and control devices for reactors. The production and use of radioactive isotopes in a variety of fields. This work was made easier by the country’s significant reserves of readily available uranium, which already had been found despite some 80 percent of the country remaining unprospected. The reactors: Even before José Cabrera was supplying power to the grid, JEN had brought several other reactors on line. Most notably, there was JEN-1, a 3-MW pool-type reactor, and CORAL, an experimental fast breeder reactor. Beyond these experimental facilities and José Cabrera, NN lists as many as seven more commercial nuclear power reactors that were on the horizon in Spain. The ultimate fate of those reactors, of course, is a much longer story best left to another TBT. Go deeper: For a closer look at the earliest days of Spain’s nuclear sector, read the full piece in the September 1968 issue of NN.
