NRC addresses NEPA proposed rule in public meeting

Changes that Nuclear Regulatory Commission officials have labeled as significant could be coming to the agency’s environmental review regulations if its proposed rule on National Environmental Policy Act procedures moves forward. The Ratepayer Protection Pledge: Who’s signed on?

In March, seven tech companies and hyperscalers signed on to President Trump's Ratepayer Protection Pledge, a voluntary agreement stating that, in the pursuit of data center growth, signatories would not shift costs to electricity customers in the form of higher electricity bills. Since then, even more entities have signed on, with states, utilities, data center developers, and cooperatives pledging support. According to the White House’s pledge list, the latest number includes 281 signatories and 23 states. Go deeper on Nuclear Newswire.

ANS to OMB: Grant rule would hinder nuclear research exchange

The American Nuclear Society submitted comments earlier this month to the Office of Management and Budget’s Office of Federal Financial Management, urging the OMB to reconsider a proposed rewrite of federal grant regulations that ANS said could restrict scientific publishing, conference participation, and long-term nuclear research. Go deeper on Nuclear Newswire.

TBT: Fort St. Vrain, in pictures

Construction on the Fort St. Vrain nuclear generating station began in November 1965, shortly after its owner/operator, the Public Service Commission of Colorado (PSCO) and its vendor, General Atomics, entered into a contract with the Atomic Energy Commission. The site’s most unique feature was undoubtedly its 330-MWe high-temperature, gas-cooled reactor, which entered operation on December 1973, a full six years after it received its operating license from the AEC. Sixteen years later, in 1989, the plant was taken off line to address an issue with struck control rods. During that shutdown, further issues were found. The cost of repairing these issues was deemed insurmountable, and the reactor never restarted. Diving into the collection: This brief recap of the story of Fort St. Vrain, of course, glosses over countless details, many of which are more fully captured in a unique, six-part series on Nuclear Newswire. That series, through dozens of pictures gathered from an extensive press package, flyers, and papers, visually lays out the story of Fort St. Vrain from the earliest days of the project up to its commercial start-up: Part 1 focuses on the history predating the nuclear plant, with maps, pictures of the trading post after which the plant would be named, and the headquarters of PSCO. Part 2 shows off a scale model of the plant, diagrams of its steam supply system, various reactor cutaways, and a one-fifth scale model of the reactor vessel. Part 3 includes fuel element diagrams, a rare photo of the fuel elements being manufactured, and a photo of the plant’s fuel handling machine being tested. Part 4 dives into flow diagrams, a steam generator schematic, a prototype helium circulator, a sophisticated half-scale HTGR model, and steam generators being fabricated. Part 5 shifts into the construction phase: site preparation, water line installation, the completed head assembly, the generator pedestal under construction, and a flag being raised on the reactor building. Part 6 celebrates the completed plant: cooling towers, the electric switchyard, the visitor center, and the fully built plant from a distance. And even more: If you are still looking for more on the story of Fort St. Vrain, another article takes a closer look at the history of the site after initial startup—along with the broader history of gas-cooled reactors—on Nuclear Newswire.
