LANL’s ZiaCore microreactor reaches criticality

Last week, Los Alamos National Laboratory announced that its ZiaCore microreactor was brought to zero-power criticality at high temperatures in April through May of this year during four weeks of experiments on the Deimos test bed at the National Criticality Experiments Research Center. Droughts, wildfires continue to impact power plants in Europe

The effects of this summer’s historic heatwave continue to be felt across Europe. Soaring temperatures and drought in the region have led to elevated river temperatures, low river levels, and wildfires, which have significantly impacted nuclear power plants across the continent. Go deeper on Nuclear Newswire.

GAO report urges DOE-EM to learn from other countries

Several countries, including Canada, France, and the United Kingdom, recently have taken steps to accelerate the cleanup of nuclear waste. In a new report, the U.S. Government Accountability Office said that these actions should serve as lessons for the United States. Go deeper on Nuclear Newswire.

ANS webinar looks at radiation studies and living near nuclear plants

In February, a study published in Nature Communications found a correlation between proximity to nuclear power plants and rates of cancer mortality. ANS recently hosted a webinar taking a deeper look at what that study (and other similar proximity studies) got wrong, the radiation science behind what we do and do not know, and why these conversations matter. Go deeper on Nuclear Newswire.

Nuclear trivia—reveal

The answer: Not including current commission members, William Anders served the shortest term of service on the Nuclear Regulatory Commission. Anders, who was sworn in on January 19, 1975, served as chairman of the first commission. He would ultimately resign from the commission on April 20, 1976. The total length of his term was about one year and three months. Prior to his stint with the NRC, Anders already had a varied and illustrious career. He had served as commissioner to the Atomic Energy Commission and as executive secretary of the National Aeronautics and Space Council. He also was a crew member on the Apollo 8 mission, the first crewed mission to orbit the moon. A deeper look at the story of Anders’s life is available on Nuclear Newswire. The longest: On the other end of the spectrum, Kristine Svinicki is the longest-serving commissioner in NRC history. She became a commissioner in 2008 and chairman in 2017, serving for nearly 13 years until her resignation in January 2021. A long-standing ANS member, Svinicki has received three Presidential Citations from the American Nuclear Society over the past two decades. Her most recent citation, awarded in November 2020, honored the work she did to guide the NRC through the COVID-19 pandemic. And more: These pieces of trivia are drawn from two sources in the August issue of Nuclear News: the feature article “The transformation of the NRC: 50 Years of Commissioners” and this month’s “Atoms” section. The data points offered in each of these pieces offer countless opportunities for much more difficult NRC-related trivia.
