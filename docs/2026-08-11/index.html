Onkalo SNF repository passes safety assessment

The “world’s first industrial-scale final disposal facility for spent nuclear fuel,” planned for the Finnish island of Olkiluoto, has received a favorable safety assessment from the Radiation and Nuclear Safety Authority of Finland. Ilkka Poikolainen, the president and CEO of Posiva, the waste management company that has been developing the Onkalo facility for decades, characterized the assessment as “a highly significant and positive milestone in the operating license process.” Go deeper on Nuclear Newswire.

Nuclear trivia—reveal

The answer: Since the formation of the Nuclear Regulatory Commission in 1975, 42 people have served as NRC commissioners. Over the years, the commission has been led by 20 chairmen. There are no specific qualifications for service on the commission, and as such, the commissioners have had disparate backgrounds. With that in mind, a recent Nuclear News feature analyzed the histories of those 42 people who have served in the role to find their commonalities and divergences. That analysis found that the most commonly held trait among NRC commissioners (at 74 percent) is education in a technical background, which was defined in this context as holding at least a bachelor’s degree in science, engineering, or health physics. The next most common characteristic is political experience at the federal level—serving either in the White House, in a congressional office, or as a political appointee in an executive agency. In the early decades of the NRC, that number was generally in the 20 percent range, but that has changed in the past 30 years. Since 1996, 14 out of 20 commissioners (70 percent) have had a political background. Rarer traits: The two least common characteristics measured in this analysis are tied at roughly 7 percent of the 42 members: membership in the National Academies of Engineering or Sciences and—perhaps more surprisingly—experience at a national laboratory. Go deeper: In the full feature, NN takes a closer look at several other characteristics, how they compare to the smaller subset of NRC chairmen, and the traits of the current commission. It is now available on Nuclear Newswire.
