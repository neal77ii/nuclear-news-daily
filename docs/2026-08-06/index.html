Uranium prices steady as EIA releases annual market report

The end-of-July spot price for uranium was $86.36 per pound, as reported by Cameco—roughly the same as it has been since February. Analytics firm Trading Economics reported a uranium futures price of $86.60 per pound, a figure that also has been holding steady since early April. These updates come as the U.S. Energy Information Administration has released its 2025 Uranium Marketing Annual Report, which examines in detail multiple aspects of the U.S. uranium market. Go deeper on Nuclear Newswire.

TBT: The first AP1000

The first Westinghouse AP1000 pressurized water reactors only started entering commercial operation in the past eight years—relatively recently in comparison to many of our other historical explorations. However, the history of the reactor’s first deployment stretches back more than two decades. A close examination of its recordings throughout the years of Nuclear News reveals some interesting facts to keep in mind as Westinghouse now aims to deploy dozens more AP1000s in the near future. Highlighted below are some of the most interesting reports on Sanmen from the archives of NN. August 2004: Sanmen, a county in China’s Zhejiang Province, appeared in NN for the first time with the report that China National Nuclear Corp. was preparing to invite bids for two 1,000-MWe power reactors at a site there. NN added that the Sanmen project—which was projected to cost $2.4 billion and eventually host a total of six units, was “part of the government’s renewed nuclear construction program designed to meet surging electricity demand in the country’s booming coastal areas.” January 2007: After a prolonged bid process involving “stiff competition” from France and Russia, China and the U.S. signed an MOU committing the country to Westinghouse and the AP1000 for Sanmen. September 2007: Westinghouse signed the final contracts to build four reactors in China: two at Sanmen and two at Haiyang. Construction was slated to begin in 2009, and Sanmen-1 was slated to enter operation in late 2013. The contracts gave Westinghouse its first orders for the AP1000 design. May 2009: Initial concrete was poured for Sanmen-1. The initial pour for Sanmen-2 (as reported in January 2010) was completed in 42 hours and 18 minutes, involving over 5,000 cubic meters of concrete, 950 metric tons of reinforcing steel, and 1,000 anchor bolts. September 2011: The world’s first AP1000 reactor pressure vessel—manufactured by South Korea’s Doosan Heavy Industries and Construction, arrived at Sanmen. The project remained on its timeline to enter commercial operation by 2013. Reports on Sanmen were few and far between for the next seven years. A November 2014 article stated that the construction of Sanmen-1 was “over 99 percent complete” and that it was expected to be connected to the grid by the end of 2015. However, technical and regulatory delays related to Sanmen’s FOAK nature continued to stretch that timeline further. June 2018: Fuel loading finally began at Sanmen-1, followed by initial criticality (reported in July 2018) and commercial operation (reported in October 2018).
