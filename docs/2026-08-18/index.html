Study mapping U-10Zr porosity informs metallic fuel design

Researchers at INL and MIT have conducted a three-dimensional study of irradiated U-10Zr—uranium alloyed with 10 percent zirconium—across key radial regions, providing new insights into how the material swells, transfers heat, and interacts with the fuel cladding. U-10Zr was extensively tested in historical sodium-cooled fast reactors and is now attracting attention for use in next-generation advanced reactors. Go deeper on Nuclear Newswire.

Nuclear trivia—reveal

The answer: The five countries that make up nuclear fuel partnership known as the Sapporo Five are Canada, France, Japan, the United Kingdom, and the United States. The group draws its name from the city of Sapporo, Japan. There, in 2023, during the G7 Ministers’ Meeting on Climate, Energy, and Environment, representatives from each country signed a nuclear fuel security agreement. That agreement aimed “to recognize and leverage the unique resources and capabilities possessed by each country’s civil nuclear sectors to establish a global commercial nuclear fuel market” and was created to prevent a nuclear fuel supply crisis for the involved countries. Revisiting today: Since the foundation of the Sapporo Five, the international focus on the nuclear fuel supply chain has grown more pronounced. The involved countries still aim to break their dependence on Russian nuclear fuel. The United States, for instance, passed the U.S. Prohibiting Russian Uranium Imports Act of 2024. Meanwhile, all five countries are aiming to deploy significant new nuclear capacity, driving projected demand higher amid a shrinking supply. Go deeper: Last week, Natalia Amosova and Seth Grae revisited the idea of front-end nuclear fuel supply cooperation and the potential benefits of allied interdependence through the Sapporo Five. That exploration is available on Nuclear Newswire.
