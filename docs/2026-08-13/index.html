Porous tungsten scrubbed by glow discharge cleaning

Researchers conducted experiments in Princeton Plasma Physics Laboratory’s Lithium Tokamak Experiment-Beta (LTX-β) showing glow discharge cleaning can be used to effectively clean samples of porous tungsten—used to hold liquid lithium in fusion machine inner walls—manufactured from powder-reconstituted materials. Slovakia’s newest reactor achieves criticality

The fourth unit of Slovakia’s Mochovce nuclear power plant has achieved criticality, operator Slovenské Elektrárne has announced. Unit 4 reached the milestone at 10:10 p.m. local time on August 6. Twenty-one minutes later, the reactor reached its minimum controlled power level. Go deeper on Nuclear Newswire.

TBT: SONGS, briefly

California has been home to four or five nuclear power plants, depending on whether or not you include the 20-MWt Sodium Reactor Experiment, which ran from 1957 to 1964. By the beginning of the 21st century, however, the state only had two left: San Onofre Nuclear Generating Station (SONGS) and Diablo Canyon. Humboldt Bay and Rancho Seco (California’s other two nuclear plants, whose stories are better left for another TBT) were shut down in 1976 and 1989, respectively. SONGS-1 was the second true commercial reactor to come on line in California, preceded only by Humboldt Bay. As chronicled in the June 1967 issue of Nuclear News, plans for the plant were publicly announced in April 1960, with an application sent to the Atomic Energy Commission in February 1963 and a permit issued in March 1964. The first unit entered commercial operation in January 1968. SONGS would go on to operate as a single-unit plant for the next 15 years, until being joined by Unit 2 in 1983 and Unit 3 in 1984. Unit 1 was shut down in 1999 after 25 years of operation and 11 fuel cycles, while Units 2 and 3 soldiered on into the 21st century. However, in 2012, significant issues were discovered in both remaining units at the plant. A year later, the plant’s operator, Southern California Edison, formally announced that it would retire SONGS rather than move forward with the needed and costly repairs, leaving Diablo Canyon as the last standing nuclear power generation site in our most populous state. Go deeper: For a less abridged look at the story of SONGS, a more detailed write-up is available on Nuclear Newswire.
